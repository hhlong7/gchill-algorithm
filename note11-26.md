so up to nov 26th,

we already have finished with stage a, which are the tool, they are set up and we have checked them w a small spike in spikes/

- SimGrid 4.1: a kernel takes max(compute, bytes / bw), and kernels on the same DRAM link share it. Changing a link's bandwidth mid-run and pausing/resuming a kernel both work, which is what gchill's DRAM control needs.

- SCALE-Sim 3.0.0: a 256^3 GEMM on the 32x32 array takes 20,351 cycles (80.5% utilization) and gives its DRAM traffic.

- Ramulator 2.1 (DDR4-2400): with 1/2/4/8/16/32 kernels streaming at once, DRAM delivers

- gem5 25.1 with OpenBLAS 0.3.34 sgemm on Neoverse V2: a 256^3 sgemm takes 637/324/173 us on 1/2/4 cores (52.7 GFLOP/s on 1 core). On DDR4 with a cold cache it takes 742 us.

- Chameleon 1.4.0 on StarPU 1.4.3: Cholesky and LU (getrf_nopiv) task graphs exported from FxT traces.
- Stage B1, the workloads, is done:
- JSON DAG format and importers for torch.export, Chameleon traces and mixes.
- Suite: ResNet-50, BERT-base, Cholesky 8x512 and 16x256, LU 8x512, and 3 mixes.
- Every workload is 29-160x the 12 MB LLC, so its data can't stay on chip and DRAM contention matters.
- A whole DAG now runs on SimGrid (`spikes/simgrid_dag.py`):
- The policy is FIFO: gemm/bgemm go on the accelerator, the rest on the CPU.
- Every kernel runs once, after all its deps.
- With unlimited DRAM, the makespan matches a plain Python replay exactly, so the simulator's timing is right.
- At 19.2 GB/s, the ML workloads slow down 2.1-4.6x (DRAM-bound). Cholesky and LU stay at about 1.0x (compute-bound).
- Mixes slow down 1.2-2.8x, which is where DRAM-aware scheduling has the most room to help.
- Costs are placeholders for now; the kernel table (B2) replaces them.

=> The tools can model what gchill needs: DRAM contention, giving one kernel DRAM priority while it runs, and the loss curve.
